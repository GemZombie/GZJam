# A Mixxx DJ's review (2026-10-21)

A fellow DJ who plays a weekly dance with Mixxx (4 decks, on Linux) sent this list of what he
uses in Mixxx, or wishes it had. It shaped the roadmap in [FEATURES.md](../FEATURES.md)
("Roadmap: getting ready for other DJs"). The status is kept up to date below. Once every item is
built or decided, this file is just a record.

## Where each item stands (as of 0.6.0, 2026-10-24)

### Built

| His item | In GZJam |
|---|---|
| Multiple decks; seeing the next track at a glance | Two or four decks. The queue sits beside the library the whole time (his reason for not using Mixxx's queue: he can't see it from the library view). Auto-play runs the queue. |
| Headphone cue on each deck; previewing in the library | A cue button on each deck, and a preview player on every song in the library, queue and history. |
| Meters on each deck and the main output, with a peak indicator | A level meter on each deck (#2). The speakers' meter holds a peak marker for 1.2 s and has a LIMIT light. |
| Master gain | A speaker volume slider and a safety limiter. |
| Headphone gain and a dry/wet knob | Headphone volume, and the phones mix (#3), which blends from what's cued to what the room hears. |
| Mono mixdown | On by default. It covers the headphones too. |
| Tempo slider with key lock | Built. Tap tempo too (#11). |
| Hot cues (jump to the outro at the end of a phrase) | Cue keys (#1): up to four per song. You pick the moment to go, and it works on air. Mixxx hot cues come across in the import (#36). |
| Main cue point after applause on live tracks | Silence and applause at either end are found automatically, or you set the start and end by hand. Both markers can be dragged (#12). |
| Ternary rating (unrated, heart, broken heart) | As described. |
| Energy rating | Low, medium or high, searchable (`energy:`). |
| `bpm:`, `~bpm:`, `crate:`, `artist:`, `year:` | All there, with `label:` in place of `crate:`. How near `~bpm:` reaches can be set (#7). |
| Crates as labels that don't look like playlists | Labels show as chips, can be renamed, and appear on the decks and queue (#13). Mixxx crates import as labels. |
| History playlists | Every night is saved as a session. Sessions can be named, and set lists exported. Mixxx history imports. |
| One-knob compressor for old recordings | EVEN in each deck's EQ panel (#5), saved per song, working around the song's own loudness. |
| Sweepable mid EQ | The mid band moves from 200 Hz to 5 kHz in third-octave steps, kept in the EQ panel as he suggested (#4). |
| ALSA direct at the dance, PipeWire at home | Picking a sound card uses it directly, so nothing else can play on it. "Default" goes through the sound server and stays shared. **Not yet tried on real Linux hardware (#19).** |

### Built differently, on purpose

- **EQ:** three bands, saved per song rather than knobs you ride live: an EQ chip on each deck,
  with "Less hiss" as a one-tap starting point. Fixing a noisy record transfer is a property of
  the recording, so it's right every time the song is loaded.
- **Locking history:** no lock needed. Past sessions are kept automatically and nothing writes to
  them; a night only keeps growing if GZJam restarts within three hours. Naming covers "rename".
- **One deck:** at least two, since the crossfader and the protection against loading over a song
  on air are built around two sides.

## The email

> Stuff I use in Mixxx for Hot Jam or wish Mixxx had:
>
> # Playback
>
> - Multiple decks are nice, with Mixxx I use 4 (currently playing, on-deck, and some alternates
>   or possible future stuff), but really all I need are one deck and way to visibly see and
>   select the next track, and possibly some way to queue up alternatives or future tracks. Mixxx
>   does have a queue where you can load up a list of tracks and even have them auto-play one
>   after the other, and I know a lot of social dance DJs on the Mixxx forums cue up several songs
>   and let them auto play, but I don't use it for whatever reason. Probably because I can't see
>   what I have queued up at a glance without changing away from the library view, so I just use
>   multiple decks.
> - Pre-fader listen (aka headphone cueing) for each deck and/or a way to quickly preview tracks
>   in the library/queue without actually loading them to a deck. Mixxx calls this a "preview
>   deck", but the exact implementation isn't important.
> - Per deck + main output vumeters and a peak indicator is nice to have.
> - Having a 3-band EQ in software is nice. I turn down the highs a lot on tracks recorded from
>   records that have a lot of high-pitched fuzz. I do this on Hot Jam's mixer, so if you're only
>   targeting use with an external mixer this isn't necessary, but I can imagine that lots of
>   dances would just have someone plugging a laptop up to an interface and having the EQ in
>   software would be useful for them. I personally wish the mid's were sweep-able, but I suspect
>   most social dance DJs wouldn't use that and I can always do that by routing the output
>   through an external plugin, so EQ probably isn't super necessary on a first pass either way.
> - Something I wish Mixxx had is a basic one-knob compressor for older tracks that aren't
>   mastered well and have a lot of range. Again, can be done with external plugins so not the
>   end of the world.
> - Having a master gain knob is nice, but maybe this will always be done from an external mixer
>   or interface.
> - Headphone gain and a dry/wet knob (so I can hear whatever I'm previewing and what's playing
>   out of the speakers) is convenient, maybe less important for social dance stuff, but it's
>   just nice to be able to keep one ear on what the dancers are hearing while previewing.
> - Having a booth output would be nice. We don't have a monitor speaker at hot jam, but I wish
>   we did because it can be hard to hear in that space, so having a way to select separate
>   outputs for mains, headphones, and a monitor would be great. If they're all on the same
>   interface or mixer this is easy, but if you end up using a small USB-C adapter for the booth
>   outputs and your main audio out for the output to the main board or something you have to
>   think about multi-soundcard syncronization and latencies, which is a PITA, so this might be a
>   stretch goal or something.
> - For Linux in particular support for using ALSA (Linux's kernel level sound API) directly and
>   PipeWire (the current most-popular userspace sound API) would be nice. At Hot Jam I use ALSA
>   because this lets me give Mixxx exclusive access to the audio interface so that some random
>   program can't accidentally play a notification sound or whatever over the speakers. At home I
>   use PipeWire because I just want to listen to music but I also want everything else to still
>   have access to my speakers so I don't have to close Mixxx before I switch to watching a video
>   or what not.
> - Mixxx has a mono mixdown mode which I always use for Hot Jam. I have a handful of tracks where
>   the beat is almost exclusively in the right speaker, so I don't want the dancers on that side
>   of the floor to be able to hear it and the dancers in front of the left speaker to not be
>   getting any drums or bass or what not, so I always just do mono output.
> - I use the tempo slider a lot at hot jam on faster tracks if the dancers look like they're
>   struggling, being able to bump it down a few BPM is nice. I also use the key-lock feature to
>   pitch the key back up or down to match so it doesn't sound weird.
> - I don't think anyone else I know does this, but I use hotcues pretty regularly at Hot Jam. For
>   example, last week I was playing Kansas City Riffs for the snowball or something, which is a
>   great track but my version is like 5 minutes long. I have a hotcue setup like a minute from
>   the end so I waited until we were a minute or so into the track and then when it came to the
>   end of a phrase I jumped to the outro cue. Similarly Mixxx has a separate concept for marking
>   the main cue point at which a song should come in, I use this to mark the end of applause in
>   live tracks and what not so that I start roughly on the music and don't have to guess.
>
> # Library
>
> - Some sort of rating in the metadata. Mixxx uses 0-5 stars. The exact implementation isn't
>   important to me personally, though I always preferred the way Zune's did it back in the day
>   with a ternary rating system (unrated, heart, broken heart) as I think the 5 star thing tends
>   to become a bit of an inconsistent mess and all I really want to know is "did I mark this as
>   'I'd play this for a dance'".
> - A thing Mixxx doesn't have that I wish it had is a second rating for "energy level". This
>   could be the same as the stars except a different emoji to make it obvious which is which in
>   the library view. Just some way to mark a track as "high energy", "low energy", etc.
> - I use Mixxx's search query language heavily, the main ones I use are:
>   - "bpm" and "~bpm" (which searches for within a configurable percentage of the BPM you
>     specify, eg. "~bpm:140" gives you all tracks in the currently selected playlist that are
>     within 10% in either direction of 140bpm).
>   - "crate" which is Mixxx's concept of unordered playlists where you can't duplicate tracks,
>     effectively "tags" or "labels". So I search for "crate:Lindy AND crate:'Female Vocals'" or
>     what not a lot.
>   - Various metadata search tags like "artist" and "year".
> - I like that Mixxx differentiates between Crates (unordered, unique) and Playlists (ordered,
>   non-unique), personally. People on the forums complain that they can't figure out the
>   difference between the two, so maybe crates should just be "labels" and displayed in a
>   different way so they don't look identical to playlists (if both are even needed for social
>   dance).
> - History playlists are nice. Mixxx keeps a new playlist ordered by date every time you start
>   the program. I normally rename and "lock" past ones from a night when I was DJing so that I
>   can remember what I played at hot jam last week and try to not overlap too much.
> - A thing Mixxx doesn't have that I'd like is some way to group multiple tracks that are
>   different recordings of the same song. I'd love to be able to group eg. a live version and a
>   studio version of a track to keep them together as one "virtual track" or something when
>   searching the library, then I could pick between them when I want to play that track. Not
>   sure how this would work, just something I've thought about implementing once or twice.
