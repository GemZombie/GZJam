A free DJ app for social dance nights.

Swing, West Coast, blues, ballroom and more. Play whole songs, choose them for the floor, and keep the music going without surprises. Simple, intuitive and useful: what you need to play music for dancers, without the clutter.

Download GZJamFor Windows, Mac and Linux

Free and open source, for Windows, Mac and Linux.

What it does

Your folders are the library

Nothing to import, and GZJam never changes your music files unless you ask it to. A request on a dancer's USB stick plays without leaving GZJam.

Two decks, or four

A crossfader for the room and a headphone cue on each deck. Mono by default, so the whole floor hears the whole band. Compact decks leave more of a laptop screen for your music.

One laptop is enough

No sound card? A splitter cable carries both: the room on the left, your headphones on the right. With a sound card, the room and your headphones each get their own output, and a booth monitor can have one too.

Loudness matching

A quiet 1938 transfer and a loud remaster play at about the same level, and GZJam says in plain words what it did to each song. A meter on each deck shows a song's level before the room hears it.

A room EQ for each venue

Tame a hall that booms or a room that swallows the warmth: three bands or ten, on the speakers only, set while the music plays. Next time, just choose where you are.

Every song ready for the floor

Songs start at their first note, and a long outro fades where you choose. Cue keys, jumps, tempo with key lock, BPM detection, and EQ for each song, with EVEN suggested for an uneven old recording.

Search made for DJs

Find songs by tempo, energy or your own labels, and leave out what the room heard last week: bpm:130-150, -played:7. Mark the keepers with a heart.

A queue that plays itself

Auto-play with a gap of real silence between songs, and when the queue runs out beside the clock. For classes and practice nights, fade each song out after a set time, wait for you, or stop on time.

Nothing interrupts the music

Songs play from memory, so a slow drive or a pulled USB stick can't stop one. It takes two taps to pause the song the room hears, and unplugged headphones never send it to the speakers. After a crash or restart, the queue and decks come back as they were, paused.

Playlists and History

Plan a set ahead of time, in folders, and keep every night's set list, to export as text or a spreadsheet.

Bring your music with you

Ratings, crates and playlists from Mixxx; playlists and star ratings from MediaMonkey, foobar2000, iTunes and most other players; and your own Navidrome or other Subsonic server, with its songs kept on the laptop before the night, so nothing depends on the Wi-Fi.

Your keys, your fingers

Choose your own keyboard shortcuts for every action, or play by touch: everything works by finger, with a touch mode for bigger keys.

That's the short version. Everything it does, and what's coming next, is on the roadmap. It plays MP3, FLAC, WAV, AIFF, OGG, Opus and Apple Lossless everywhere, AAC on Windows and Mac, and WMA on Windows.

The first time you open it

GZJam is free and isn't signed with a paid certificate, so your computer may warn you once.

Windows

If it says “Windows protected your PC”, click More info, then Run anyway.

Mac

If it says it can't check GZJam for malicious software, open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway beside the note about GZJam.

At startup GZJam asks GitHub whether there's a newer version, and says so before the music starts. That request sends nothing about you or your music, and you can turn it off in Settings.
